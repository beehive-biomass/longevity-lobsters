Design system specification
The lobster is the longevity animal: it never stops growing, and it grows by molting — shedding the old shell, soft for a while, then harder than before. Every surface here is built on that picture. Warm sand under deep-water ink, kelp green for what is alive, tide blue for what is measured, and one coral action per screen.

Guidelines · the rules every screen keeps
The molt is the metaphor. A protocol is a molt: shed, soften, harden. Progress is drawn as rings, never as a bar that fills to 100%, because a lobster is never finished growing.
One question at a time. The intake is eight screens of one question, never one form of eight fields. A clear way back on every one.
Colour never carries meaning alone. Every chip has a word, every gauge a unit, every line on a chart a label and a dash. Take the colour away and it still reads.
The shell is drawn, not explained. Held data wears the lock in slate; data you chose to share wears the eye in tide blue. The two never sit in one list.
A gauge that cannot vouch says so. "Not measured yet", "not connected" — never 0, never a dash, never a dimmed button. What is not available yet is a plain row with its reason.
Research status, never a claim. A peptide page shows the trial sponsor, the phase and the date as a chip with its source. No outcome language. Hemp and cannabinoid pages cite the same way.
Consent before the price. Sealing the shell shows the storage price, what becomes shareable and what stays held on the same screen, then the check, then the one coral button.
Edges, not shadows; one tide line, no gradients. The tide line at the foot of a page is the only decoration; it is drawn, original, and takes the colour of its words.
